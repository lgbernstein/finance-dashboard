Daily Snapshot — June 9, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Stock futures point slightly higher this morning, but two big risks loom: fresh Iran tensions and tomorrow's inflation report. A calmer start follows Monday's volatility. The market's direction this week depends on the May inflation number due Wednesday and any news from the Israel-Iran conflict.

Overnight and This Morning

Markets are set for a quieter open after a choppy Monday. US stock futures, which are bets on where stocks will open before the regular session begins, are pointing slightly higher. S&P 500 futures are up about 0.2% and Nasdaq 100 futures are up about 0.6%. That means the market is expected to open a touch higher, led again by chip and AI stocks.

The bounce follows a strong day for chipmakers on Monday. Micron, a memory-chip maker, rose close to 10% after dropping 13% on Friday. Stocks closed mixed Monday: the S&P 500 finished at 7,405.73, up 0.3%, and the Nasdaq rose 0.86%, while the Dow slipped 0.16%.

Bond yields stayed firm. The 10-year Treasury yield, the benchmark interest rate that drives mortgages and stock valuations, sits near 4.57%. Higher yields keep borrowing costs up and put a ceiling on how high stocks can climb.

Top Stories Today

Iran tensions flare again President Trump said the US "must respond" to recent Iran retaliation, raising the risk that US military strikes resume soon. This rattled markets late Monday and could push oil prices higher. Oil is the main driver of high inflation right now, so any escalation keeps pressure on the Fed to hold interest rates up. Watch the headlines closely.
Inflation report lands tomorrow The Consumer Price Index, or CPI, measures how fast everyday prices are rising. The May reading comes out Wednesday morning, June 10. It is the most important number of the week. A cooler reading would ease fears that the Fed might raise rates. A hot reading, especially with oil rising again, would deepen those fears just ahead of next week's Fed meeting on June 16-17.
AI data-center spending keeps flowing Corning jumped more than 9% Monday after Amazon signed a multibillion-dollar deal to buy its optical fiber, the cabling that connects data centers. This is a reminder that companies keep investing heavily in AI infrastructure even after chip stocks had their first big pullback. It supports the parts of your stock holdings tied to technology.

What to Watch Today

  • Any official news from the Israel-Iran conflict. Each headline moves oil prices, and oil moves inflation and your portfolio.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield near 4.57%. If it climbs above 4.75%, bond prices stay under pressure and stocks lose support. If it drops below 4.25%, it signals investors believe inflation is cooling.
  • Positioning ahead of tomorrow's CPI report. Markets often trade nervously the day before a big inflation number, so expect some caution.
