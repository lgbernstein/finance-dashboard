What to Watch — May 2026

30-Year Yield at 5.12%

Bond values down. Stocks under pressure.

⌄

The 30-year Treasury yield crossed 5.12% — not seen since before 2020. When it rises, existing bond values fall and new bonds pay more. For most of the last decade it sat between 2–4%.

What to watch: Above 5.5% = significant stock pressure. Below 4.75% = inflation fears easing.

Strait of Hormuz Closed

20% of global oil blocked. Prices elevated.

⌄

Roughly 20% of all the world's traded oil passes through the Strait daily. When it closes, oil rises — and higher oil pushes up the cost of almost everything.

What to watch: Diplomatic talks involving Iran. A reopening would trigger a stock rally and bond yield drop almost immediately.

Fed Rate Hike Being Priced In

Markets expect rates to rise before end of 2026.

⌄

Six months ago markets expected rate cuts. That reversed completely. Rate hikes make bonds more attractive relative to stocks and squeeze corporate borrowing costs.

Silver lining: Money market funds and short-term T-bills — already ~5% — would pay even more after a hike.

Second Wave of Inflation

Energy shock spreading into broader prices.

⌄

This second wave is driven by geopolitical disruption. The Fed cannot raise interest rates to reopen the Strait.

Stagflation risk: High inflation AND slow growth simultaneously. The hardest environment for a retirement portfolio. Analysts are now taking this scenario seriously.

Maria Still Working

Your household has a real income cushion.

⌄

Maria's income means you have the option to not touch retirement accounts at all during a downturn. The biggest danger in a down market is being forced to sell at low prices.

Strategic opportunity: If markets fall significantly, Maria's income could allow buying at lower prices — the opposite of what most retirees are forced to do.

RMDs Start in ~5 Years

Planning window is open. Use it now.

⌄

At 73 the IRS requires minimum withdrawals from traditional IRAs and 401k accounts — taxable whether you need the money or not. The five years before RMDs start are your restructuring window.

Ask your advisor: "Have we modeled our RMD amounts starting in 2031, and should we be doing Roth conversions now?"

Strait of Hormuz closed → oil restricted → oil price rises → costs up everywhere → CPI risesCPI — Consumer Price IndexMonthly report measuring price changes. The Fed targets 2% annual inflation. When CPI runs above that, the Fed feels pressure to raise rates. → bond investors demand moreBond investorsAnyone lending money to the government by buying Treasuries. When inflation is high, they demand higher rates or inflation erodes their fixed payments. → yields riseBond yieldThe annual interest rate a bond pays relative to its price. Yields and prices always move in opposite directions. → bond values fallWhy bond values fallIf you own a bond paying 3% and new bonds pay 5%, nobody pays full price for yours. Its value drops until its return matches the new rate. → stocks less attractiveStocks vs. bondsWhen bonds pay 5% with almost no risk, stocks need to offer significantly more upside to compete. Money shifts from stocks to bonds, pushing prices down. → stocks fall → Fed considers rate hikeWhat the Fed doesThe Federal Reserve raises the Federal Funds Rate to cool spending and eventually cool inflation — but with a 12-18 month lag. → housing slows, consumer spending slows, corporate profits squeezed.

The chain reverses if the Strait reopens — oil drops, inflation fears ease, yields fall, stocks recover. That is why geopolitical news moves markets so fast.

Why Natural Gas Matters Now

Fertilizer link: About 80% of the cost of nitrogen fertilizer (urea, anhydrous ammonia) is natural gas. When gas spikes, fertilizer costs spike — and food prices follow 6–12 months later.

Electricity: About 40% of US electricity comes from natural gas. Higher gas prices mean higher utility bills for homes and businesses.

Europe: Europe is far more gas-dependent than the US. Prolonged Middle East disruption puts European energy security at risk, which moves global gas markets.

LNG exports: 100% of US production gains are exported. More LNG terminals = tighter domestic supply = higher bills.

⚡ Energy Supply Monitor — Matt Randolph (Mr. Global)

Key indicators tracked by oil & gas expert Matt Randolph — 33 years in the industry, owns an operating oil company. Source: @MrGlobalYouTube. Each card includes implications and how to assess the reading.

WTI Crude Oil Price
$62/bbl
$70 — new well break-even floor
$60 — existing well profitability floor
Below new well break-even — drilling stops
Implication: At $62, oil companies will not drill new wells — it costs more than they make. "Drill Baby Drill" is economically impossible at this price. The US needs ~$70/bbl to incentivize new drilling. 100% of US production gains since 2016 have been exported anyway — so even if drilling resumed, domestic consumers see no price benefit.
How to assess: $60–$70 = no new drilling but existing wells continue. Below $60 = some existing wells go offline too. Above $70 = new drilling resumes in 6–12 months. Above $90 = full drilling economics return. Watch rig count (Baker Hughes) as the 2-week-lagging confirmation.

Implication: The US needs 7,000–8,000 new wells per year just to hold production flat — because every existing well declines every day from day one (the best day is always day one). US Strategic Petroleum Reserve
~340M bbl
Current release: 1.22M b/d (record)
Gap it's filling: ~10% of disruption
Record release rate — still can't fill the gap
Implication: Even at a record 1.22M b/d release, the SPR plugs only ~10% of the 12–13M b/d disruption. Critical distinction: SPR "loans/exchanges" do not suppress oil prices — the market expects that oil back, so it stays priced in. Only outright sales suppress spot prices. Congress banned SPR sales to China in 2022 (law still stands). At current draw rate, the reserve runs ~280 days — but functional mechanisms can break before then.
How to assess: EIA publishes SPR levels weekly. Watch the trend: if the weekly drop is accelerating, depletion is sooner than arithmetic suggests. Below 300M bbl = serious. Below 250M bbl = critical. Also watch whether releases are classified as loans vs. sales — only sales create actual price suppression. Loan = price signal to the market that oil is coming back off the market.

Henry Hub Natural Gas
~$2.50/MMBtu
Now: spring lull, mild demand
Summer + LNG exports → price spike
Deceptively quiet — watch June–August
Implication: Natural gas drives most US electricity generation, especially in the Northeast. Spring prices look calm — but they are seasonal. Once summer heat arrives and LNG export facilities ramp up, domestic supply tightens rapidly. Every LNG terminal sends gas overseas, which pulls domestic prices higher, not lower. Trump reversed Biden's LNG export slowdown — this benefits gas exporters, not US consumers. People in deregulated NE states (CT, MA, PA) have seen $1,200–$1,400 monthly electric bills.
How to assess: Henry Hub below $3 = stable bills. $3–$5 = elevated. Above $5 = utility bill shock. Watch: (1) summer temperature forecasts for the NE corridor, (2) LNG export terminal utilization rates, (3) storage injection/withdrawal pace vs. 5-year average. If LNG exports are rising AND a hot summer arrives simultaneously, bills spike hard.

Bab-el-Mandeb Strait
Watch
Connects Red Sea to Indian Ocean
If closed: second chokepoint gone
Open but at risk — escalation path is close
Implication: If Bab-el-Mandeb closes alongside Hormuz, two of the world's critical maritime arteries fail simultaneously. Shipping reroutes add weeks. Insurance and freight triple. At that point, we are no longer discussing recession — we are discussing a depression on the scale of the 1930s. Gulf oil infrastructure damage (pipelines, refineries, loading terminals) is the sleeper risk: it can be destroyed in minutes but takes years to rebuild. Saudi Arabia's Abqaiq facility alone processes 7% of global oil supply. A 2019 drone strike temporarily knocked out 5% — prices jumped 15–20% in one day.
How to assess: Houthi attack frequency in the Red Sea is the leading indicator. Any confirmed strike on Abqaiq or major Gulf infrastructure = immediate price spike + years of elevated prices. Two straits closing simultaneously = scenario outside normal financial modeling. Watch for: Saudi Arabia security briefings, Houthi ceasefire talks, US carrier group positioning in the Red Sea.
Food Price Inflation
4-yr High
Fastest rise in 4 years — now
Oil → fertilizer → shipping → food
Oil shock is transmitting into food prices
Implication: Food prices rising at a 4-year high is the first visible consumer sign that the oil supply shock is spreading. The chain: high fuel costs raise fertilizer costs (fertilizer is made from natural gas), which raises farming costs, which raises shipping costs, which raises grocery prices. For US households this compounds utility bill pressure. For developing nations that were already food-insecure, this path leads to famine — not a prediction, a supply-chain formula.
How to assess: USDA Food Price Outlook and CPI food sub-index (separate from headline CPI). If food inflation persists even after oil stabilizes, second-round inflation effects are embedded — the Fed cannot ignore this and may have to raise rates even into a slowing economy (stagflation). Stagflation is the hardest environment for a retirement portfolio: bonds fall, stocks fall, cash buys less. Food prices are the earliest consumer-visible sign of energy-driven inflation becoming structural.
