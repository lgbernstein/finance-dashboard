Daily Snapshot — June 11, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Stocks point higher this morning after US strikes on Iran ended quickly. Yesterday was a rough day. Today is calmer so far, but the May inflation report came in hot, so the mood can shift fast.

Overnight and This Morning

Markets are pointing up this morning. S&P 500 futures are up about 0.7%, and Nasdaq futures are up about 1.3%. Futures are bets on where the market will open before trading begins. Up futures mean stocks are expected to open higher. The S&P 500 is the main index that tracks 500 large US companies, so it is a good gauge of the overall market.

The lift came after the latest round of US strikes on Iran ended quickly. That eased some fear and helped calm oil prices. The bounce follows a hard day on Tuesday, when the S&P 500 fell 1.62% to 7,267 on Iran worries and a hot inflation report.

Bond yields held steady. The 10-year Treasury yield sat near 4.55%. A Treasury is a loan you make to the US government. The yield is the interest it pays. When yields rise, prices of existing bonds fall, which can dent the bond part of your accounts.

Top Stories Today

Inflation hit a three-year high, but the details are less scary Consumer prices rose 4.2% over the past year, the fastest since 2023. Inflation is the rate at which prices rise. Almost all of the jump came from oil and energy, not from everyday goods across the board. Core inflation, which strips out food and energy, rose just 0.2% in May. That was lower than expected. The takeaway: this looks like an oil problem tied to Iran, not a broad price surge.
Iran strikes ended fast, easing oil and stock fears The latest US military action against Iran wrapped up quickly. That calmed oil prices and let stocks recover some of yesterday's losses. Oil matters to you because higher oil feeds inflation, and inflation can push the Federal Reserve to keep interest rates high. Watch for any reversal, since this story can turn quickly.
The Fed meets next week, and a hold looks near certain The Federal Reserve sets short-term interest rates that ripple through the economy. It meets June 16 and 17. This is the first meeting under new Chair Kevin Warsh. Traders put roughly 96% odds on no change, leaving rates at 3.50% to 3.75%. The hot inflation number all but ended hopes of a near-term rate cut.

What to Watch Today

  • Oil prices. If the Iran calm holds, oil should ease and stocks can stay firm. A flare-up would push oil and inflation worries back up.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield, now near 4.55%. If it climbs above that, bond prices stay under pressure and the bond side of your accounts can slip.
  • Next week's Fed meeting on June 16 and 17. No rate change is expected, but Chair Warsh's tone on inflation will set the mood for stocks and bonds.
