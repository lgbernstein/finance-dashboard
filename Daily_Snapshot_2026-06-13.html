Daily Snapshot — June 13, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Markets are closed this weekend. The big event is next week: the Federal Reserve meets Tuesday and Wednesday. The Fed sets the interest rate that ripples through your savings, your bonds, and the stock market. Its Wednesday decision is the main thing to watch.

Where Things Stand

It is Saturday, so US stock and bond markets are closed. They reopen Monday morning. Here is where they finished the week.

Stocks rose on Friday. The S&P 500, an index that tracks 500 large US companies and is the standard measure of the overall market, gained 0.5% to close at 7,431. The Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.7%, and the Nasdaq, which leans toward technology companies, rose 0.3%. A calm, positive end to the week.

Bond yields ticked up. The 10-year Treasury yield, the interest rate the US government pays to borrow money for 10 years, rose to about 4.5%. When this yield rises, the price of bonds you already own tends to fall. It moved up partly on fresh uncertainty about a possible US deal with Iran.

Top Stories Today

The Federal Reserve meets next week, with a decision Wednesday afternoon The Fed sets short-term interest rates for the whole economy. It has held its rate steady at 3.50% to 3.75% so far this year. Most expect another hold on Wednesday. The bigger news will be the Fed's updated forecast for how many rate cuts to expect, which moves both stocks and bonds.
The Iran conflict is keeping energy prices and inflation in focus The ongoing Iran war has pushed up oil prices, which raises the cost of gas and many goods. The Fed now expects inflation, the rate at which prices rise, to end the year near 2.7%, a bit higher than before. If the conflict cools, those price pressures could ease. This is the main wild card for markets right now.
SpaceX went public Friday in the largest stock listing ever Elon Musk's rocket company started trading at $150 a share and jumped about 19% on its first day. An IPO is when a private company first sells shares to the public. This one drew heavy attention and lifted the overall market mood, though a single new stock does not change the picture for your retirement accounts.

What to Watch This Week

  • The Federal Reserve meeting Tuesday and Wednesday, June 16 and 17. The rate decision and new forecasts come out Wednesday at 2 PM Eastern. This is the week's main event for both stocks and bonds.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield, now near 4.5%. If it keeps climbing, bond prices stay under pressure. If it falls back, bonds you own gain value.
  • Any news on the Iran conflict and oil prices. A step toward calm would ease inflation worries. More tension would push energy costs and yields higher.
