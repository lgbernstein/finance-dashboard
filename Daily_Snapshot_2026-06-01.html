Daily Snapshot — June 1, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Stocks open June at record highs on a tech rally, but oil jumped as Iran halted talks with the US. A new oil spike keeps inflation pressure alive, and that is what would push the Fed toward higher rates, the main risk to both your stocks and your bonds.

Overnight and This Morning

Markets start the new month on a strong note. US stock futures are pointing higher. Futures are contracts that bet on where the market will open, so a rising future means stocks are expected to open up. S&P 500 futures are up about 0.3%, and Nasdaq futures are up about 0.3%, building on May, which closed at record highs.

The move is led by technology. Nvidia, the largest chipmaker, rose more than 2% before the open after unveiling a new processor for personal computers. That single stock is pulling the whole market up. On the other side, oil prices climbed overnight after Iran's state news agency said Iran suspended talks with the United States over Israel's actions in Lebanon and Gaza. Higher oil is the reason bond yields stayed elevated, with the 10-year Treasury yield near 4.5%. A Treasury yield is the interest rate the US government pays to borrow; when it rises, existing bonds you own lose value and mortgage rates go up.

Top Stories Today

Stocks reach new record highs to start June The S&P 500, the index of 500 large US companies, and the Nasdaq are both at all-time highs. The S&P 500 is on track for a ninth straight positive week, the longest run since 2023. Strong company earnings are behind it. About 85% of large companies beat profit forecasts last quarter, well above the usual rate. This is good for your stock holdings.
Oil rises as Iran suspends talks with the US Iran halted negotiations with the United States, citing Israel's military actions. Oil prices moved higher in response. This matters because oil is the main driver of high inflation right now. Cheaper oil lets prices cool. A fresh spike keeps inflation hot and gives the Federal Reserve, the US central bank that sets interest rates, a reason to hold rates high or even raise them.
US factories show their best month in years A widely watched measure of factory activity, the ISM manufacturing index, rose to 54 in May, better than expected. A reading above 50 means factories are growing. This signals the economy is solid, which supports stocks and reduces near-term recession worries.

What to Watch Today

  • Any official word from the US or Iran on the suspended talks. Each headline moves oil, and oil moves inflation and your portfolio.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield, now near 4.5%. If it stays above that level, bond prices stay under pressure and money market funds keep paying around 4.5%, an attractive safe return.
  • The May jobs report due Friday, June 5. It will shape what the Fed does next. A weak number raises recession talk; a strong one supports holding rates high to fight inflation.
