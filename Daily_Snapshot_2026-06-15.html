Daily Snapshot — June 15, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  End of Day
Stocks jumped after the U.S. and Iran announced a deal to end their conflict. A peace agreement eased war fears and pushed oil prices down. That helps both stocks and your retirement accounts, since lower oil tends to mean lower inflation.

Overnight and This Morning

Markets rallied hard today. The S&P 500, the index that tracks 500 large U.S. companies and is the main yardstick for the stock market, rose 1.65% to close at 7,554.29. The gain came after President Trump announced an agreement to end the war between the U.S. and Iran and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow shipping lane that carries much of the world's oil.

The reaction in oil was the big story. Crude oil fell more than 5% to about $80 a barrel, a two-month low, as traders bet that Persian Gulf shipments would soon resume. Lower oil feeds through to cheaper gasoline and less pressure on prices overall. Bond yields eased on the same news. The 10-year Treasury yield, the interest rate the U.S. government pays to borrow for 10 years and a key benchmark for mortgages and savings, slipped to about 4.47%, down from roughly 4.59% a month ago.

Top Stories Today

U.S.–Iran peace deal lifts stocks, sinks oil The announced agreement aims to end the conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz by week's end. Markets cheered the lower war risk. For you, the main benefit is cheaper oil, which calms inflation worries and supports both stock and bond prices.
Deal terms are not yet confirmed Neither government has released the written agreement. Iranian media published a draft with different terms, and Trump said it did not match what was agreed. Shipping companies are holding off on sending vessels through the route until things are clearer. The rally could give back gains if the deal wobbles.
Federal Reserve meets tomorrow and Wednesday The Fed, the U.S. central bank that sets short-term interest rates, holds its two-day meeting June 16 and 17. Markets put the odds of no rate change at about 99%, leaving the rate at 3.5% to 3.75%. The decision and the Fed's comments come Wednesday afternoon.

What to Watch Today

  • Fed meeting begins Tuesday. The rate decision and outlook land Wednesday afternoon. No change is expected, so the wording about future cuts will matter more than the decision itself.
  • Oil prices. If crude holds near $80 or falls further, that is good news for inflation and your accounts. A bounce back above $90 would signal the deal is in doubt.
  • Any official text of the U.S.–Iran agreement. Confirmation would support today's rally. Signs the deal is unraveling could reverse it quickly.
