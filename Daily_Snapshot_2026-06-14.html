Daily Snapshot — June 14, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Markets are closed for the weekend. They head into a big week on a calm, hopeful note. A possible U.S. and Iran peace deal pushed oil prices down on Friday. Lower oil eases worries about inflation, which is good for both stocks and bonds.

Where Things Stand

It is Sunday, so the U.S. stock and bond markets are closed. They reopen Monday morning. Here is the picture from Friday's close.

Stocks finished a strong week on a quiet note. The S&P 500, which tracks 500 large U.S. companies and is the main gauge of the overall market, ended Friday roughly flat. The Dow Jones, an index of 30 big companies, rose about 0.6%. The Nasdaq, which leans heavily on technology stocks, slipped about 0.3%. The big move came Thursday, when the S&P 500 jumped 1.75% to about 7,394 after President Trump signaled a peace deal with Iran was close.

Bond yields eased. The yield on the 10-year Treasury note, a government IOU that sets the tone for mortgage and loan rates, finished the week near 4.49%, down from 4.55% the week before. When yields fall, existing bond prices rise, so this was a small plus for the bond side of your portfolio.

Top Stories Today

U.S. and Iran move toward a peace deal President Trump said he canceled planned strikes on Iran and that the two sides reached what he called a strong memorandum of understanding, an early-stage written agreement. The news cooled tensions in the Middle East. For your accounts, a calmer Middle East usually means steadier markets.
Oil prices fell, easing inflation fears Oil dropped on the peace news. Cheaper oil means cheaper gas and lower costs across the economy. That eases inflation, the rate at which prices rise. Lower inflation pressure takes some heat off the Federal Reserve, the central bank that sets interest rates, and helps both stocks and bonds.
The Fed meets this week, and it is the new chair's first meeting The Federal Reserve, the central bank that sets interest rates, meets Tuesday and Wednesday. This is the first policy meeting led by new chair Kevin Warsh. Most economists expect no change to rates, which sit at 3.5% to 3.75%. With inflation still high at 4.2% a year, what Warsh says about the path ahead will matter more than the decision itself.

What to Watch This Week

  • The Federal Reserve meets Tuesday and Wednesday, June 16 and 17. No rate change is expected. Watch new chair Kevin Warsh's tone. If he sounds worried about inflation, bond yields could rise. If he sounds patient, markets should stay calm.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield, now near 4.49%. A drop below 4.25% means investors believe inflation is cooling. A move above 4.75% signals renewed worry. This level shapes the value of your bonds.
  • The Iran peace deal. If it is signed as expected, oil should stay low and markets calm. If talks fall apart, expect oil to climb back toward $100 and market swings to return.
