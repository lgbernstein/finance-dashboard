Daily Snapshot — May 22, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh takes over today as markets head for their best weekly streak since 2023. Warsh inherits elevated inflation and bond yields near multi-year highs. His first remarks will signal where interest rates may go next, which directly affects bond values and borrowing costs.

Overnight and This Morning

US stock futures are up about 0.2% this morning, pointing to a slightly higher open. That means the market is expected to continue its recent run, which is on pace for the longest streak of weekly gains since 2023. The S&P 500 (a broad index that tracks 500 large US companies) closed yesterday at 7,445. The Dow Jones Industrial Average (a well-known index of 30 major US companies) set a new all-time record at 50,285.

Bond yields are holding near elevated levels. The 10-year Treasury yield (the interest rate the US government pays to borrow money for 10 years, often used as a benchmark for mortgages and other loans) sits at 4.61%. The 30-year yield eased slightly to 5.08%. When yields are high, existing bond prices fall, which can weigh on the bond portion of a retirement portfolio.

Top Stories Today

New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh Sworn In Today Kevin Warsh officially becomes the 11th chair of the Federal Reserve today, replacing Jerome Powell. The Fed chair has enormous influence over interest rates, which affect everything from your bond portfolio to savings account returns. Warsh inherits core inflation at 2.8% (still above the Fed's 2% target), the 30-year Treasury yield recently above 5%, and a strong economy. Markets will watch his first public statements closely for any hint of whether rates will stay put, rise, or eventually fall.
Stocks on Track for Best Weekly Streak in Three Years Despite concerns about bond yields and global economic weakness, US stocks have been climbing. Iran peace negotiations are reportedly in their "final stages" per President Trump, which has helped calm oil markets. Oil prices have been volatile around $96 to $100 per barrel. Lower oil prices tend to ease inflation pressure, which is good news for consumers and potentially for the Fed's rate decisions.
Global Manufacturing Slumping; US Factory Survey Misses Badly The Philadelphia Fed's manufacturing index (a monthly survey of factory activity in the mid-Atlantic region) came in at -0.4 this week against an expected reading of 17.6. A negative number means factories in that region are contracting. European service sector surveys also fell below 50 in France and the UK, which signals contraction. These weak readings suggest the broader economy may be slowing even as the stock market holds up.

What to Watch Today

  • University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment (final May reading) is due today. This monthly survey measures how confident Americans feel about their finances and the economy. A weak reading could signal that consumers are pulling back on spending, which would affect corporate earnings and potentially stock prices.
  • Watch the 10-year Treasury yield around 4.6% to 4.7%. If it pushes higher, bond prices in your portfolio will face more pressure. If it retreats, that is a sign of easing rate fears and a positive for bonds.
  • Kevin Warsh's first public remarks as Fed Chair. Any hint about the future path of interest rates will move both stocks and bonds. If he sounds hawkish (meaning inclined to raise rates to fight inflation), expect yields to tick up and stocks to pull back. A cautious or neutral tone would likely be welcomed by markets.
