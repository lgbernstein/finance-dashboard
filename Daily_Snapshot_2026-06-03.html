Daily Snapshot — June 3, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Stocks pull back from record highs as oil jumps on renewed Middle East tension. Oil drives inflation, and higher inflation keeps interest rates high. That combination pressures both your stocks and your bonds, so today is a watchful day rather than a calm one.

Overnight and This Morning

Yesterday the S&P 500, the index of the 500 largest US companies and the standard measure of the stock market, closed at a record high of 7,609.78. That was its first close above 7,600 and its ninth straight winning day. This morning the mood has shifted. Stock futures, which are bets on where the market will open, point lower across the board. The S&P 500 is set to open down about 0.7%, the Dow about 0.9%, and the Nasdaq, the tech-heavy index, about 1.1%.

The trigger is oil. West Texas Intermediate, the main US oil price, jumped 3.2% to about $96.73 a barrel overnight as tensions in the Middle East flared again. Brent, the global benchmark, rose to about $98.80. Big technology names like Microsoft are leading the pullback. The 10-year Treasury yield, the benchmark interest rate that sets mortgage and bond pricing, edged up to 4.46%. When this yield rises, the value of bonds you already own slips.

Top Stories Today

Oil spikes back near $100 on Middle East tension US crude rose 3.2% overnight and global crude is near $99. Oil is the single biggest reason inflation has stayed high this year. Higher oil feeds into gasoline, shipping, and food prices, which keeps overall inflation elevated and gives the Federal Reserve, the US central bank that sets interest rates, less room to lower them.
OECD cuts its global growth forecast The OECD, a research group funded by major economies, lowered its 2026 world growth estimate to 2.8% from 3.4% last year. It warned that continued disruption to oil shipping could push growth even lower. Slower global growth can weigh on company profits, which matters for stocks sitting at record levels.
Markets now lean toward a rate hike, not a cut Traders are pricing in roughly a 70% chance the Fed raises interest rates by year-end, a reversal from earlier bets on cuts. Higher rates pressure bond values you already hold. The upside: cash, money market funds, and short-term Treasuries still pay around 4.5%, a safe and attractive return.

What to Watch Today

  • Oil headlines. Any news out of the Middle East will move crude prices fast, and oil is the main driver of inflation right now. A further spike keeps rate-hike worries alive.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield, now at 4.46%. If it pushes above 4.75%, bond prices stay under pressure. If it falls back below 4.25%, it signals investors believe inflation is cooling.
  • The May jobs report due Friday, June 5, and the May inflation report due June 10. Both feed directly into the Fed's June 16-17 meeting, which sets the tone for the rest of the month.
