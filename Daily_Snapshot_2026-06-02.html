Daily Snapshot — June 2, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Stocks pull back slightly after Monday's record highs, with rising oil and Middle East tension in the mix. All three major indexes hit record closes Monday. Futures point a touch lower this morning, so a calm but watchful day for your stock holdings.

Overnight and This Morning

Markets are taking a small breather. After all three major US stock indexes closed at record highs on Monday, stock futures are pointing slightly lower this morning. Futures are contracts that let traders bet on where the market will open, so they give an early read on the day. S&P 500 futures are down about 0.2%, meaning the market is expected to open a little lower than where it closed.

The S&P 500 is the main index that tracks 500 large US companies. It closed Monday at 7,599.96. The Dow Jones Industrial Average, a 30-company index, set a record at 51,078.88. Bond yields held steady. The 10-year Treasury yield sits near 4.47%. A Treasury is a loan you make to the US government, and the yield is the interest it pays. When that yield stays high, mortgages and other loans stay expensive. Oil prices climbed after Iran suspended indirect talks with the United States, which is the main reason the mood is more cautious today.

Top Stories Today

Records set, but warning signs underneath Monday's record highs were driven by a small group of technology stocks, led by Nvidia, which rose 6% on a new computer chip. When only a few stocks carry the market, the gains can be fragile. One valuation gauge, the Shiller P/E ratio, now sits at its second-highest level since 1999, which means stocks are expensive by historical standards. This does not predict a drop, but it is a reason to avoid chasing this rally.
Strong manufacturing data supports the economy A key US factory survey, the ISM Manufacturing index, came in at 54.0, a four-year high. Any reading above 50 signals factories are growing. This is good news. It suggests the economy is still expanding, which supports company profits and, over time, your stock and bond holdings.
Oil rises on Middle East tension Iran suspended indirect negotiations with the United States in protest of Israel's military actions in Lebanon. Oil prices jumped in response. Higher oil feeds into inflation, which keeps the Federal Reserve from cutting interest rates. That matters for both your bond holdings and the broader market.

What to Watch Today

  • The Federal Reserve meets June 16 and 17. The Fed sets the short-term interest rate that influences savings, loans, and mortgages. Markets expect no change, with the rate held at 3.5% to 3.75%. Today, watch for any Fed official comments that hint at the next move.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield near 4.47%. If it stays at or above this level, bond prices stay under pressure and mortgages stay expensive. A move higher would weigh on both stocks and bonds.
  • Oil prices and Middle East headlines. If tension escalates, expect more market swings. A calming of the situation would likely ease oil and steady stocks.
