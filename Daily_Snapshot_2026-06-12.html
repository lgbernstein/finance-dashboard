Daily Snapshot — June 12, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Stocks rise again as a US-Iran peace deal looks close, possibly signed this weekend. A deal would lift oil sanctions and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, the shipping lane that carries much of the world's oil. Lower oil means lower inflation, which eases pressure on interest rates and helps both your stocks and your bonds.

Overnight and This Morning

Markets are pointing higher again. S&P 500 futures, which are bets on where the main US stock index will open, are up about 0.6% before the bell. That follows a strong day Thursday, when the S&P 500 rose 1.75%, the Nasdaq jumped 2.54%, and the Dow gained nearly 2%. Stocks are on track for a winning week.

The driver is hope for peace. President Trump said the US and Iran could sign an agreement as soon as Sunday. A draft reportedly has the US lifting oil sanctions and Iran reopening the Strait of Hormuz within 30 days. On that news, crude oil fell about 2% to near $85 a barrel, and the 10-year Treasury yield, the benchmark interest rate that drives mortgages and stock values, slipped to about 4.47%, down roughly a tenth of a point. A lower yield lifts the value of bonds you already own.

Top Stories Today

US-Iran peace deal may be signed this weekend A draft agreement would have the US lift oil sanctions and Iran reopen the Strait of Hormuz within 30 days. This is the single most important development for your portfolio. The war drove oil and inflation higher for months. A real deal reverses that, which is why stocks are rising and oil is falling.
SpaceX goes public today in a $75 billion debut SpaceX begins trading on the Nasdaq today, one of the largest stock market debuts ever. Retail investors alone placed more than $100 billion in orders. You do not need to act on this. It matters mainly as a sign of how much money is flowing into technology and artificial intelligence stocks right now.
Wholesale prices ran hot, a reminder inflation is not beaten Wholesale prices, what producers charge before goods reach stores, rose 6.5% over the past year, the fastest in nearly four years. This is the cost pressure still working through the economy from the oil shock. It is the main reason the Federal Reserve, the US central bank that sets interest rates, is in no hurry to cut rates even as markets rally.

What to Watch Today

  • University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment for early June is out today. This survey measures how confident households feel about spending. Economists expect a small rebound to 46 from 44.8. A higher number suggests people feel better about the economy.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield near 4.47%. If it keeps falling, bond investors believe inflation is cooling and your bond values rise. If it turns back up above 4.6%, rate worries are returning.
  • Any confirmation or breakdown of the Iran deal over the weekend. A signed deal would extend the rally. A collapse would send oil back up and undo this week's gains.
