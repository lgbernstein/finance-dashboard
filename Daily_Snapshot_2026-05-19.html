Daily Snapshot — May 19, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Bond yields surge to multi-year highs as chip stocks lead markets lower for third straight day. Rising yields push down bond prices and squeeze technology stocks, both of which affect a typical retirement portfolio.

Overnight and This Morning

US stock futures are pointing lower again this morning. S&P 500 futures are down, putting the index on track for its third consecutive losing session. The Nasdaq 100 (an index heavy in technology stocks) fell roughly 1% in pre-market trading.

The bigger story is in the bond market. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.63%, its highest level since early 2025. The 30-year Treasury yield climbed to around 5.18%, the highest it has been in nearly 19 years. When yields go up, bond prices go down. That means bonds currently held in retirement accounts are worth a bit less on paper than they were a month ago. This is a trend worth watching closely.

Top Stories Today

Chip stocks in a two-day slide, dragging tech lower Semiconductor stocks (companies that make computer chips) dropped sharply again. Seagate fell nearly 7% and Micron Technology dropped 6% after a Seagate executive said new factories would "take too long" to build. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, a broad measure of chipmakers, is now down more than 6% over two days. This matters because technology stocks make up a large share of most S&P 500 index funds.
Inflation forecast jumps to 6% for Q2, raising fear of another Fed rate hike Top economic forecasters now project inflation (the rate at which prices rise) will hit 6% in the second quarter of 2026. That is a significant jump and would be the highest in roughly three years. Markets now put a 40% chance on the Federal Reserve (the US central bank) raising interest rates again this year, after previously expecting no changes. Higher rates tend to slow the economy and pressure both stocks and bonds.
Trump calls off planned Iran attack; Middle East tensions ease for now President Trump announced he canceled a planned military strike on Iran, saying "serious negotiations" toward a peace deal are underway. This reduced some of the risk premium that had been pushing oil prices higher. Lower oil prices, if sustained, would help ease inflation pressures somewhat.

What to Watch Today

  • Home Depot reports earnings this morning. Its results will signal whether American consumers are still spending on home improvement, which is a useful gauge of overall consumer health. A weak report could add more pressure to stocks.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield is the key number to follow. If it pushes above 4.65% today, bond prices will face further downward pressure. Watch for any move toward 4.75% or higher, which would be a new milestone and likely trigger more selling in stocks.
  • Kevin Warsh was just confirmed as the new Federal Reserve Chair. Markets will scrutinize any early public comments from him for clues about whether he is more inclined to raise rates or hold steady given rising inflation.
