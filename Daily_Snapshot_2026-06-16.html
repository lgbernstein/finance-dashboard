Daily Snapshot — June 16, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Markets are calm and steady as the Federal Reserve begins a two-day meeting. The Fed sets short-term interest rates, which affect the income you earn on savings and bonds. A decision comes tomorrow afternoon, so today is mostly a waiting day.

Overnight and This Morning

Markets are quiet this morning after a strong day on Monday. US stock futures, which are bets on where the market will open, are holding roughly flat. That means stocks are expected to open near where they closed.

The calm follows a relief rally. On Monday the S&P 500, an index that tracks 500 large US companies, rose 1.65% to close at 7,554. The jump came after the US and Iran agreed to end hostilities and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a key shipping lane for oil. Oil prices fell on the news, which helps ease the cost of fuel and shipping. The 10-year Treasury yield, the interest rate the US government pays to borrow for 10 years and a benchmark for mortgages, sits near 4.47%, down slightly from earlier this month.

Top Stories Today

The Federal Reserve meets today and tomorrow, its first under new Chair Kevin Warsh The Fed is widely expected to leave its key interest rate unchanged at 3.50% to 3.75% when it announces tomorrow at 2:00 PM Eastern. Traders do not expect any rate cuts this year. Steady rates keep bond income and savings yields where they are, which is generally fine for a retirement portfolio.
Oil prices stay low after the US-Iran deal The agreement removed a major worry that had hung over markets for weeks. Lower oil helps slow inflation, which is the rate at which prices rise. Cooler inflation supports both stocks and bonds, and it is part of why mortgage rates have eased. The average 30-year mortgage rate is now 6.37%, down from 6.62% a month ago.
Bank of Japan raised its key rate to 1.00% Japan's central bank is slowly moving away from years of near-zero rates. This rarely moves US markets directly, but it can cause swings in currencies and global bonds. Worth noting, not worth worrying about.

What to Watch Today

  • The Fed decision lands tomorrow at 2:00 PM Eastern, followed by Chair Warsh's first press conference. His tone matters more than the rate itself, since it hints at where rates go next.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield near 4.47%. If it keeps drifting lower, bond prices rise and mortgage rates ease further. If it climbs back up, the opposite happens.
  • Whether Monday's calm holds. After a big one-day jump, markets often pause. A quiet, flat day is normal and not a cause for concern.
