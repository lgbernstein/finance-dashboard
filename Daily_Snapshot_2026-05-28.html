Daily Snapshot — May 28, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Stocks hit fresh records despite hottest inflation in 3 years The S&P 500 rose to a new all-time high this morning even after a government report showed prices rising at their fastest pace since 2023, leaving the Fed (Federal Reserve, which controls interest rates) in a difficult position heading into summer.

Overnight and This Morning

US stock futures were little changed overnight, but once markets opened the S&P 500 climbed about 0.4% and the Nasdaq (the index heavy in technology companies) rose about 0.5%, both pushing into new all-time high territory. The Dow Jones Industrial Average was slightly lower, off about 0.6%.

Bond yields, which move in the opposite direction of bond prices, remain elevated. The 10-year Treasury yield (the interest rate the US government pays to borrow money for 10 years, widely used as a benchmark for mortgages and corporate loans) sits at 4.50%. The 30-year Treasury yield hit 5.2% this month, its highest level since 2007. Higher yields put pressure on bond prices, which matters for the portion of your retirement accounts held in bonds.

Top Stories Today

Inflation at a 3-year high: PCE report released this morning The government released its April PCE report today. PCE (Personal Consumption Expenditures) is the Fed's preferred way to measure inflation, meaning it carries extra weight in interest rate decisions. Prices rose 3.8% compared to a year ago, up from 3.5% last month. The main driver is war-related oil price increases from the US-Iran conflict, plus ongoing tariffs on imported goods. Americans are also drawing down savings to keep up with rising costs, with the personal saving rate falling to 2.6%, the lowest since mid-2022.
S&P 500 shrugs off inflation data, hits new record Despite the hot inflation report, stocks continued climbing. The S&P 500 is up roughly 0.4% midday, reaching a new all-time high. Goldman Sachs raised its year-end S&P 500 price target to 8,000, citing strong corporate earnings growth. For context, a rising stock market is good news for the stock portion of your and Maria's retirement accounts.
Iran ceasefire extension: markets watching closely Reports circulated earlier this week that negotiators reached a deal to extend the ceasefire between the US, Israel, and Iran. Markets rallied on the news, though traders remain cautious since similar reports have proven unreliable in recent weeks. Oil prices and inflation remain elevated as long as the conflict continues. Any confirmed deal would likely push oil prices lower and ease inflation pressure.

What to Watch Today

  • PCE inflation report is the main event today and has already been released. Markets are digesting it now. Watch whether stocks hold their gains through the afternoon, which would suggest investors believe the inflation surge is temporary.
  • The government also reports GDP (Gross Domestic Product, the total size of the US economy) and weekly jobless claims today. If jobless claims rise sharply, that would add to concern that the economy is slowing even as inflation stays high, a combination called stagflation that is particularly hard for the Fed to address.
  • Earnings after the close today: Dell Technologies and Dollar Tree report. Dollar Tree results in particular can signal how lower and middle-income Americans are handling higher prices.
