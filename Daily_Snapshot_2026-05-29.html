Daily Snapshot — May 29, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Stocks set to close out a strong May at record highs as oil keeps falling. A calm, positive morning. Cooling oil prices and a softer inflation reading have eased pressure on both stocks and bonds, which is good for your retirement accounts.

Overnight and This Morning

Markets are quietly positive heading into the final trading day of May. US stock futures point slightly higher: Dow futures are up about 0.3% and S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures are up about 0.1%. Futures are contracts that bet on where the market will open, so this means stocks are expected to open a touch higher. Both the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq closed at record highs yesterday, the second record close in a row. The S&P 500 finished Thursday at 7,563.63.

Bond yields eased again overnight. The 10-year Treasury yield, the benchmark interest rate that influences mortgages, loans, and how investors value stocks, dropped to about 4.44%, its lowest level in more than two weeks. That is down from a 16-month high of 4.7% reached on May 20. Falling yields lift the value of bonds you already own, so this is a positive move for the bond side of your portfolio. The drop comes from reports of a tentative US-Iran ceasefire deal, which lowers the risk of an oil-driven jump in inflation.

Top Stories Today

Oil keeps falling on US-Iran peace optimism West Texas Intermediate crude, the main US oil price, fell about 1.4% to $87.66 a barrel. Brent, the global benchmark, slipped to $92.47. The drop follows reports of a 60-day US-Iran ceasefire memorandum. Lower oil feeds directly into lower gas and energy prices, which cools inflation and is good for both stocks and bonds.
Inflation reading came in softer than expected Yesterday's PCE report, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, showed monthly price increases below what economists expected. The annual numbers remain high at 3.8% headline and 3.3% core, both still above the Fed's 2% target. Core strips out food and energy to show the underlying trend. A cooler monthly reading is a step in the right direction and helped push bond yields down.
Dell jumps on strong AI server demand Dell stock is rising sharply after an earnings report showed booming demand for its artificial intelligence servers. This continues the tech-led rally that carried the market to records this week. If you hold an S&P 500 or total-market index fund, you own a slice of these gains.

What to Watch Today

  • No major US economic data is scheduled for release today. The next big report is the May Consumer Price Index on June 10, which will show whether this week's oil drop is cooling inflation.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield at 4.44%. If it stays below 4.5%, bond prices should keep firming. A move back above 4.75% would signal renewed inflation worry.
  • Whether the US-Iran ceasefire memorandum gets formally signed and published. A signed deal pushes oil lower and stocks higher. A breakdown would reverse this week's gains quickly.
