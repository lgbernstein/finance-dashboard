Daily Snapshot — May 31, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
A calm weekend after stocks closed May at record highs. Markets are closed today (Sunday). The S&P 500 and the Dow both finished Friday at all-time highs, so you start the new week from a position of strength.

Overnight and This Morning

Markets are closed for the weekend. They reopen Monday morning, June 1. There is no live trading today and no futures activity yet, so this is a quiet look back and a look ahead.

Stocks ended May on a high note. On Friday the S&P 500, the broad index of 500 large US companies that most retirement accounts track, closed at a record 7,580. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed above 51,000 for the first time ever, at 51,032. That capped the ninth straight week of gains, the longest winning streak of 2026. The 10-year Treasury yield, the benchmark interest rate that drives mortgages and bond prices, eased to 4.44%. That is down from a 16-month high of 4.7% reached on May 20, which is good news for the bonds you already own, because bond prices rise when yields fall.

Top Stories This Weekend

Stocks close May at record highs The rally was led by technology and AI-related companies. Dell jumped about 30% on Friday after strong earnings tied to demand for AI computing hardware. Goldman Sachs raised its year-end forecast for the S&P 500 to 8,000, citing steady corporate profit growth. For your accounts, this means stock holdings ended the month near their highest values on record.
Oil keeps falling on the US-Iran deal, but it is not signed Reports describe a preliminary framework to extend a truce and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow shipping lane that carries a large share of the world's oil. Oil has slid from above $110 a barrel toward $91 over recent weeks. President Trump has not formally endorsed the terms yet. Cheaper oil eases inflation, which helps both stocks and bonds. A breakdown in talks would reverse that quickly.
Consumer confidence dipped slightly in May A monthly survey that measures how households feel about the economy slipped to 93.1 from 93.8 in April. This is a small move, not a warning sign. It is worth noting only because consumer spending drives most of the US economy, so this number is one to track over the coming months.

What to Watch This Week

  • Markets reopen Monday morning, June 1. Your full weekly briefing arrives Monday at 8 AM.
  • The May jobs report comes out Friday, June 5. It shows how many jobs the economy added and the unemployment rate. A strong number supports stocks but could keep interest rates high for longer.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield at 4.44%. If it stays below 4.5%, bond prices hold steady or improve. A move back above 4.75% would signal renewed inflation worry and pressure on bonds.
  • Any official word on the US-Iran deal. A signed framework pushes oil lower and lifts markets. A collapse sends oil back up and stocks down.
