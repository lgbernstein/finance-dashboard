Daily Snapshot — June 8, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Stocks are trying to bounce back after Friday's sharp drop, but two new worries are pulling in opposite directions. A strong jobs report has pushed up interest rates, and weekend fighting between Israel and Iran has rattled energy markets. Both touch the value of your stocks and bonds.

Overnight and This Morning

Markets are unsettled but recovering this morning. After a rough Friday, US stock futures, which are bets on where the market will open, swung around overnight and now point slightly higher. The S&P 500, an index that tracks 500 large US companies and is a good gauge of the overall market, is up about 0.8% in early trading. The tech-heavy Nasdaq is up around 1.5% as chip stocks recover.

The big mover is interest rates. The yield on the 10-year US Treasury bond, the interest the government pays to borrow for ten years and the benchmark that sets rates across the economy, climbed to about 4.54%, its highest in two weeks. Higher yields tend to weigh on stocks, especially fast-growing technology companies, because borrowing costs more. When bond yields rise, the prices of bonds you already own fall.

Top Stories Today

Strong jobs report cools hopes for lower interest rates The US economy added 172,000 jobs in May, far above the roughly 85,000 forecast. Unemployment held at 4.3%. A strong job market is good news, but it means the Federal Reserve, the US central bank that sets interest rates, has less reason to cut them. Traders now see a real chance the Fed could even raise rates by December. That pushed bond yields up and stocks down on Friday.
Israel and Iran trade missile strikes over the weekend The two countries exchanged attacks, and Israel struck Iranian petrochemical sites on Monday. Oil and gold prices moved on the news. Iran later said it had ended its military operation, and President Trump signaled optimism about a deal, which helped calm markets this morning. Conflict in the Middle East matters to you because it can raise energy prices and add uncertainty for stocks.
Chip stocks rebound on big new deals Semiconductor companies, which make the chips that power computers and AI, are leading the morning bounce after Friday's steep fall. Corning jumped about 9% after Amazon signed a multibillion-dollar deal for fiber to power its data centers, and Marvell rose about 9% on news it will join the S&P 500. This matters because chip and tech companies make up a large share of the index your retirement funds likely track.

What to Watch Today

  • No major US economic data is scheduled today. The next big event is the Federal Reserve meeting on June 16 and 17, the first under new Chair Kevin Warsh, where the Fed is widely expected to hold rates steady.
  • Keep an eye on the 10-year Treasury yield. If it stays above 4.5%, bond prices will remain under pressure and high-growth tech stocks may stay shaky.
  • Watch the Israel-Iran situation. If the fighting flares up again, expect oil prices to rise and stocks to wobble. If calm holds, this morning's rebound has room to continue.
