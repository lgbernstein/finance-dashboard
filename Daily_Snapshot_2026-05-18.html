Daily Snapshot — May 18, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
10-Year Treasury Yield Hits 15-Month High as Inflation Fears Grip Markets Rising oil prices tied to the Iran conflict are stoking inflation fears, pushing bond yields to their highest level since early 2025 and keeping pressure on both bond and stock portfolios.

Overnight and This Morning

US stock futures opened lower overnight, dropping about 1% at their worst point before recovering most of those losses by Monday morning. At the opening bell, the S&P 500 (an index tracking 500 large US companies) was nearly flat, up 0.09%. The Nasdaq (tech-heavy index) gained 0.15%, while the Dow Jones slipped 0.08%. Small-company stocks had a rough start, with the Russell 2000 index falling 2.44%.

Bonds (government IOUs that pay fixed interest) continued their rough stretch from last week. The yield (interest rate) on the 10-year US Treasury note touched 4.631% before settling around 4.60%, its highest level in 15 months. When yields rise, the value of bonds you already own falls. Oil prices climbed to $106.30 per barrel for US crude, helping ease somewhat from overnight highs as positive headlines emerged on the Iran situation.

Top Stories Today

Inflation Running Hotter Than Expected The Cleveland Federal Reserve's latest estimate puts trailing 12-month inflation at 4.18% for May, well above the Fed's 2% target. Energy prices, up roughly 60% since the Iran conflict began, are the main driver. The Federal Reserve (the US central bank that sets interest rates) has put any planned rate cuts on hold. Higher rates for longer mean bond prices stay under pressure and borrowing costs stay elevated.
Global Bond Selloff Continues Bond yields rose sharply around the world last week and are holding near those highs today. Japan's 30-year government bond yield hit an all-time record. Germany's 10-year yield is at a 15-year high. For your bond holdings, higher yields mean lower current prices on bonds you already own. This affects bond funds in IRAs and 401(k)s. The effect softens over time as bonds mature and are reinvested at the newer, higher rates.
Nvidia Earnings Wednesday: A Major Market Moment Nvidia (the chip company whose products power most AI systems) reports quarterly earnings on Wednesday. The stock market has rallied significantly this year based on AI optimism. If Nvidia's results disappoint, the whole market could sell off. If they beat expectations, it could provide relief. FOMC minutes (a summary of the Federal Reserve's last meeting discussions on rates and inflation) are also due this week and will be closely watched.

What to Watch Today

  • FOMC minutes are due this week. These are the notes from the Fed's last meeting and will reveal how seriously officials are taking the inflation spike. If the minutes sound hawkish (meaning they lean toward raising rates or holding them higher longer), bond yields could rise further and stocks could dip.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield at 4.60-4.63% is a key level. If it keeps rising toward 5%, that puts more downward pressure on bond values and makes stocks less attractive compared to the safe income bonds now offer.
  • Oil prices and Iran conflict headlines: oil fell slightly this morning on reports of possible diplomatic progress. Any escalation or resolution will move markets quickly. Oil has been the trigger for most of this year's inflation surge.
