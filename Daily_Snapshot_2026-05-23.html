Daily Snapshot — May 23, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  Weekend Edition
Stocks close their 8th straight winning week, but warning signs are building. Markets ended Friday on a high note with a new record Dow. However, a new Fed Chair just took office, oil is back above $100 a barrel, and consumer confidence just hit a new low. All three things matter for your retirement accounts.

What Happened Friday (May 22)

US stock markets closed higher on Friday and finished their best 8-week winning streak in years. The Dow Jones Industrial Average (an index that tracks 30 large US companies) rose 294 points to close at 50,579, setting a new all-time record high. The S&P 500 (which tracks 500 major US companies and is a broader measure of the market) rose 0.37% to 7,473. The Nasdaq (which is heavy on tech companies) rose 0.19%.

On the bond side, the 10-year Treasury yield (the interest rate the US government pays to borrow money for 10 years, which influences mortgage rates and many other loan costs) eased slightly to 4.56%. The 30-year Treasury yield settled at 5.07%. When yields fall, bond prices rise, which is good for bond holders like you.

Note: US markets are closed this Monday, May 25, for Memorial Day. The next trading day is Tuesday, May 27.

Top Stories

New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh officially takes over Kevin Warsh was sworn in Friday as the new chair of the Federal Reserve (the US central bank that controls interest rates). He replaced Jerome Powell in a White House ceremony. The Senate confirmed him 54-45, the most divided Fed confirmation vote in history. Markets will be watching closely for any signals he gives about whether he plans to raise, lower, or hold interest rates steady. Higher interest rates tend to push bond yields up and can slow stock gains.
Oil back above $100 a barrel US crude oil prices climbed back above $100 per barrel this week. Oil above $100 tends to push gas prices higher, which feeds into overall inflation (the general rise in prices). Higher inflation makes it harder for the Fed to cut interest rates, which can put pressure on both stocks and bonds. The key variable here is whether Middle East peace talks (US and Iran are negotiating) lead to more oil supply reaching markets.
Goldman Sachs warns of a potential market correction The investment bank Goldman Sachs issued a warning this week that rising bond yields and stubborn inflation could trigger a stock market correction (a drop of 10% or more from recent highs). This comes even as markets have been hitting record highs. The disconnect between high consumer confidence in stocks and low consumer confidence in the economy is a signal worth watching. The University of Michigan survey of consumer sentiment fell to a new low this month.

What to Watch This Coming Week

  • Tuesday May 26: Consumer confidence data drops. After the University of Michigan survey showed people feeling worse about the economy, this report from the Conference Board will confirm whether that trend is spreading. Weak consumer confidence often leads to slower spending, which can slow corporate earnings.
  • Wednesday May 27: New home sales figures. Higher mortgage rates (tied closely to the 10-year Treasury yield at 4.56%) have been slowing the housing market. This number will show how much.
  • Wednesday May 28: Two big reports in one day. The government releases its second estimate of first-quarter GDP (Gross Domestic Product, a measure of total US economic output), plus April PCE prices. PCE (Personal Consumption Expenditures) is the Fed's preferred inflation gauge. If inflation looks sticky, it strengthens the case for keeping rates high longer, which is a headwind for both stocks and bonds.
  • New Fed Chair Warsh: Watch for any early signals or speeches. As a new leader with a history of preferring tighter monetary policy (meaning higher interest rates), the market will parse his every word carefully in his first weeks on the job.
