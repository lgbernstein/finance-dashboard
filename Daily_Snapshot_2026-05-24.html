Daily Snapshot — May 24, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  Memorial Day Weekend Edition
Markets post an 8th straight winning week, but a record-low consumer confidence reading and 3-year high inflation warn that the good run faces real tests ahead. Stocks have been rising steadily, yet everyday Americans feel worse about the economy than at any point on record, and prices for food, travel, and energy keep climbing.

This Weekend and What Happened Friday

Markets are closed today (Sunday) and Monday for Memorial Day. The next trading session is Tuesday, May 26. This snapshot covers where things stood when markets closed Friday and what to expect when they reopen.

Friday was a strong finish. The S&P 500 (a broad measure of 500 large U.S. companies, often used as a proxy for "the stock market") rose 0.37% to close at 7,473. The Dow Jones Industrial Average, which tracks 30 major companies, set a new all-time record high, gaining 294 points. That wrapped up the eighth straight week of gains for the S&P 500, the longest winning streak since 2023. Bond yields (the interest rate the U.S. government pays when it borrows money) eased slightly. The 10-year Treasury yield settled at 4.56%, down a hair from the day before. When yields fall, existing bond prices rise, which is a small positive for bond holdings.

Top Stories This Weekend

Iran-U.S. Peace Talks: Progress, but No Deal Yet A Qatari diplomatic team flew into Tehran Friday to help broker a deal between the U.S. and Iran, whose conflict has kept oil prices sharply elevated. Brent crude oil sits near $120 per barrel, roughly 50% above pre-conflict levels. If a deal closes, oil prices are expected to fall significantly, which would ease inflation pressure across the economy. For you and Maria, lower oil prices would help reduce the cost of goods and could give the Federal Reserve (the U.S. central bank) more room to eventually cut interest rates, which would benefit bond prices.
Consumer Confidence Falls to an All-Time Record Low The University of Michigan's May consumer sentiment survey, released Friday, showed Americans feel worse about the economy right now than at any time since the survey began. People cited rising gas prices and inflation fears. This matters because consumer spending drives roughly 70% of the U.S. economy. If people pull back on spending, corporate earnings can suffer and stocks often follow. The disconnect between the rising stock market and falling consumer mood is a tension worth watching.
Inflation at 3.8% Annually, a 3-Year High The most recent data shows prices overall are 3.8% higher than a year ago, the highest rate since 2023. Memorial Day weekend costs illustrate the pressure: ground beef is up 14.5% over the past year, a steak costs 16.1% more, airline tickets are up 20.7%, and hotel stays cost 4.3% more. The Federal Reserve (Fed) is holding its benchmark interest rate steady at 3.5% to 3.75% for now. Rising inflation makes it harder for the Fed to cut rates, and if it has to raise them instead, bond prices would fall and borrowing costs would rise.

What to Watch When Markets Reopen Tuesday

  • Kevin Warsh is expected to formally take the oath as the new Federal Reserve Chairman. Markets will pay close attention to any early signals about his views on interest rates and inflation. A hawkish (meaning leaning toward raising rates) tone could pressure both stocks and bonds.
  • Watch the 10-year Treasury yield. It sits at 4.56% now. If it climbs back above 4.7% to 4.8%, that signals bond investors are worried about inflation staying high, which adds pressure to both bonds and stocks.
  • Iran negotiations remain the wildcard. Any sign of a deal closing would likely push oil prices lower and give markets a lift. A breakdown in talks could send oil higher and rattle stocks.
