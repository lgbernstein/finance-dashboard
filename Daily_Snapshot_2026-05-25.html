Daily Snapshot — May 25, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  Memorial Day
Markets closed today for Memorial Day. Futures point strongly higher for Tuesday's open. A potential US-Iran deal to reopen a key oil shipping route drove futures up sharply overnight, which is good news for your retirement accounts when trading resumes.

Overnight and This Morning

All US stock and bond markets are closed today in observance of Memorial Day. The New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq will reopen Tuesday, May 26 at 9:30 AM Eastern.

Even with markets closed, futures contracts, which are agreements to buy or sell at a future price and trade around the clock, tell us where stocks are expected to open Tuesday. Right now they are pointing strongly higher. S&P 500 futures are up about 0.9%, and Nasdaq 100 futures are up about 1.3%. That means your stock-heavy accounts look set for a solid gain when markets open tomorrow.

The driver: overnight news that the US and Iran are close to finalizing a deal that would reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Oil prices dropped sharply on the news, with Brent crude (the global benchmark) falling over 5% to around $98 a barrel. That matters because cheaper oil means lower inflation pressure, which means less reason for the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates.

Top Stories Today

US-Iran Deal Could Reopen the Strait of Hormuz The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway off the coast of Iran that roughly 20% of the world's oil passes through. Iran closed it earlier in 2026, setting off an oil supply crisis and driving prices to painful highs. A deal to reopen it would ease that pressure. Oil is still about 50% above where it was before the conflict, so any relief matters significantly for inflation and for the cost of everyday goods.
S&P 500 Just Completed Its 8th Straight Winning Week Before the holiday, the S&P 500 (an index that tracks 500 large US companies and is a broad measure of the stock market's health) gained 0.4% last week, extending a streak of eight consecutive winning weeks. That is the longest such run since December 2023. Your stock holdings have been on a good run heading into summer.
Oil Still Far Above Pre-Crisis Levels Even with today's sharp drop, oil at $98 a barrel is still roughly 50% higher than before the Iran conflict began. That means inflation, the general rise in prices across the economy, remains elevated. The 10-year Treasury yield (the interest rate on 10-year US government bonds, which affects mortgage rates and bond values) sat at 4.56% as of Friday, still historically high. If the Iran deal falls apart, expect oil to jump back and bond yields to follow.

What to Watch Today and This Week

  • Tuesday open: Watch how S&P 500 futures hold up overnight. If futures stay elevated by Tuesday morning, your accounts should open higher. Any reversal in the Iran deal talks could flip sentiment quickly.
  • Iran deal progress: This is the single biggest market driver right now. A signed agreement would likely push oil down further and stocks up. A breakdown in talks would do the opposite.
  • PCE inflation data: The Fed's preferred inflation measure, called PCE (Personal Consumption Expenditures), is expected sometime this week. If it comes in lower than expected, that increases the chance the Fed cuts rates later this year, which is good for both stocks and bonds.
