Daily Snapshot — June 4, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
The Dow hit a new record, but chip stocks dragged the tech-heavy Nasdaq lower. Money is rotating out of technology into steadier sectors like health care and financials. Your broad stock funds should hold up well, since the strength in other sectors is offsetting the weakness in tech.

Overnight and This Morning

The story today is a split market. Stock futures, which are contracts that signal where the market is likely to open, pointed lower early after weak results from chipmaker Broadcom. By the open, the picture changed. The Dow Jones Industrial Average, an index of 30 large, established US companies, rose about 827 points, or 1.6%, to a fresh record high. The S&P 500, which tracks 500 of the largest US companies and is the best gauge of the overall market, edged up about 0.4%. The Nasdaq, which is heavy in technology stocks, lagged behind and traded near flat.

Bond yields held steady. The 10-year Treasury yield, the interest rate the US government pays to borrow money for 10 years, sat near 4.49%. When this yield rises, existing bond prices fall, so a flat yield is calmer news for the bond part of your portfolio. Oil prices eased after a ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon, which takes some pressure off inflation.

Top Stories Today

Broadcom and CrowdStrike sink, pulling chip stocks down Broadcom fell about 13% after its revenue forecast came in below what investors expected. CrowdStrike, a cybersecurity firm, dropped more than 11% on soft sales guidance. Technology was the only major sector to fall today. If you own tech-heavy funds, expect a soft patch there, but the rest of the market is picking up the slack.
Israel-Lebanon ceasefire cools oil prices A ceasefire agreement eased fears of a wider Middle East conflict. Oil prices slipped as a result. Lower oil helps keep inflation in check, which is good for both stocks and bonds over time.
Investors shift toward steadier sectors Instead of leaving the market, investors moved money from tech into health care, financials, and consumer companies. This kind of rotation is normal and is a sign of a market spreading its bets rather than panicking.

What to Watch Today

  • The May jobs report arrives Friday morning. This is the official monthly count of how many jobs US employers added. Analysts expect a modest gain of about 85,000 jobs, down from 115,000 in April. A soft number could raise hopes for lower interest rates later this year.
  • Keep an eye on the 10-year Treasury yield near 4.49%. If it climbs back above 4.5%, bond prices stay under pressure. If it eases, your bond holdings get a small lift.
  • The Federal Reserve, the central bank that sets short-term interest rates, meets June 16 and 17. Markets expect no change to rates this time. Watch for any signals about the path ahead.
