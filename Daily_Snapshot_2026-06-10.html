Daily Snapshot — June 10, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
The US and Iran traded direct military strikes overnight, and this morning's inflation report came in at 4.2%, the highest in three years. Both stories push in the same direction: higher oil, stickier inflation, and pressure on stocks and bonds at the same time.

Overnight and This Morning

The conflict escalated overnight. After President Trump said Iran shot down an American military helicopter patrolling the Strait of Hormuz, the US launched new strikes on Iran. Iran's Revolutionary Guard says it retaliated by hitting US bases in Jordan, Bahrain, and Kuwait. This is a direct exchange between the US and Iran, a step beyond the Israel-Iran fighting that has driven oil prices since February. Oil climbed again on the news, and stock futures, which show where the market is expected to open, drifted lower through the night.

Stocks opened lower this morning. The S&P 500, the main measure of the US stock market, is down about half a percent, with the Dow and Nasdaq down a similar amount. The 10-year Treasury yield, the interest rate on US government bonds that drives mortgage rates and bond prices, sits near 4.53%, roughly where it was yesterday. The drop is orderly so far, not a panic.

Top Stories Today

US and Iran exchange direct military strikes The US struck Iran overnight, and Iran hit back at US bases in three Gulf countries. Markets care because the fighting threatens the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway that carries much of the world's oil. More escalation means higher oil, and higher oil feeds the inflation that is already squeezing stocks and bonds.
May inflation hits 4.2%, the hottest reading since 2023 This morning's Consumer Price Index, the government's main measure of how fast prices are rising, showed prices up 0.5% in May and 4.2% over the past year. Energy caused over sixty percent of the monthly rise. The one bright spot: core inflation, which strips out food and energy, rose only 0.2% for the month, less than expected. That tells you the problem is the oil war, not runaway spending across the whole economy.
Rate cut hopes fade ahead of next week's Fed meeting The Federal Reserve, the US central bank that sets interest rates, meets June 16 and 17. Traders now see a roughly 97% chance it holds rates steady at 3.50% to 3.75%. With inflation at 4.2%, more than double the Fed's 2% target, rate cuts are off the table for now, and a December hike remains priced in. For you, this means money market funds and short-term Treasuries keep paying attractive safe yields, while existing bond values stay under pressure.

What to Watch Today

  • Iran headlines and oil prices. Brent crude has been trading near $97 a barrel. Each escalation headline pushes oil and inflation expectations higher; any sign of de-escalation would do the opposite, fast.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield, now near 4.53%. If it climbs above 4.75%, bond prices will fall further and stocks will feel more pressure. A drop below 4.25% would signal markets expect inflation to cool.
  • Fed officials' reaction to today's inflation report. Their comments will set the tone heading into the June 16-17 meeting, the first full test of how new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh reads an oil-driven inflation spike.
