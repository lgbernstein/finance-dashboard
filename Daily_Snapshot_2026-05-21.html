Daily Snapshot — May 21, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Iran deal talks enter "final stages" — oil prices drop sharply, easing pressure on inflation If a deal holds, energy prices should fall further, which would take pressure off inflation and make it less likely the Federal Reserve raises interest rates again.

Overnight and This Morning

US stock futures are mixed this Thursday morning. S&P 500 futures are down about 0.3%, meaning the broad market is expected to open slightly lower. But tech stocks are pointing higher: Nasdaq futures are rising after Nvidia reported blowout earnings after the close yesterday.

Bond yields, which have been the main worry for investors all week, are easing a bit. The 10-year Treasury yield (the interest rate the US government pays on 10-year loans; when this goes up, bond prices fall and borrowing costs rise across the economy) is at 4.63%, down from a 16-month high of 4.7% earlier this week. The 30-year yield briefly hit 5.2% on Monday, its highest level since 2007, but has since pulled back to around 5.1%.

Oil prices, which had been a major driver of market anxiety, are falling sharply on news that Iran negotiations may be near an end. West Texas Intermediate crude is around $97 per barrel, down from over $102 yesterday.

Top Stories Today

Iran Deal in "Final Stages" — Oil Falls, Strait of Hormuz Easing President Trump said negotiations with Iran are in "final stages," and three large oil tankers have already left the Strait of Hormuz (the narrow waterway through which roughly 20% of the world's oil passes). Oil crashed more than 5% on the news. Lower oil prices reduce inflation pressure, which is good for bonds and could give the Federal Reserve more room to hold interest rates steady rather than raising them.
Nvidia Blows Past Earnings Expectations Nvidia, the leading maker of chips used in artificial intelligence systems, reported quarterly revenue of $81 billion, up 85% from a year ago and well above analyst forecasts. The company guided for $91 billion in the next quarter. This is a big deal because Nvidia's results are seen as a barometer for AI investment broadly. Strong Nvidia numbers tend to lift tech stocks, which make up a large share of the S&P 500 and most stock market funds.
Federal Reserve Officials Warn Rate Hike Is Back on the Table Minutes from last month's Fed meeting show that "many" officials warned the central bank may need to raise interest rates if inflation stays stubbornly above their 2% target. Inflation, as measured by the PCE (Personal Consumption Expenditures, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge), is running at 3.5% annually, driven largely by high energy costs. Higher rates would put more pressure on bonds and could slow stock market gains. The Iran deal news today is directly relevant here: cheaper oil would help bring that inflation number down.

What to Watch Today

  • No major economic data releases are scheduled today, so markets will react primarily to news flow, especially any updates on Iran deal progress or oil prices.
  • Watch the 10-year Treasury yield. If it stays below 4.65% today, it signals the bond market is calming down. If it climbs back toward 4.7% or above, expect more pressure on stocks and bond funds in your portfolio.
  • Watch oil prices throughout the day. The 5% drop this morning is significant. If WTI crude stays below $100 per barrel, it strengthens the case that the inflation threat is receding and the Fed can hold rates steady.
