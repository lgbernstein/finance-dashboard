Daily Snapshot — May 26, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Iran peace talks lift stocks as markets reopen after Memorial Day. Hopes for a US-Iran deal are pushing stocks higher and oil lower, which is good news for both your stock holdings and for keeping inflation in check.

Overnight and This Morning

US markets were closed yesterday for Memorial Day. They reopen this morning with a positive tone. S&P 500 futures (contracts that predict where the stock market will open) are up about 0.5%, and Nasdaq 100 futures are up 0.8%. That means the market is expected to open modestly higher.

Overnight in Asia, Japan's Nikkei index broke above 65,000 for the first time ever, a sign that global investor confidence is strong. European markets also opened near their highest levels since March. The main fuel for all of it: oil prices dropped sharply as the US and Iran signaled progress toward a peace deal that could reopen a key shipping lane in the Middle East. When oil gets cheaper, it takes pressure off prices across the economy, which is good for stocks and bonds alike.

The 10-year Treasury yield (the interest rate the US government pays to borrow money for 10 years, which also affects mortgage rates and bond values) fell to about 4.51% this morning, down from 4.57% at the end of last week. When yields fall, the value of existing bonds goes up, which is a small positive for the bond portion of your portfolio.

Top Stories Today

Oil drops 6% as US-Iran talks show real progress President Trump said over the weekend that negotiations with Iran are "proceeding nicely." Two oil tankers cleared the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway that had been threatened by the conflict. West Texas Intermediate crude (the US oil benchmark) fell to around $90.60 a barrel. Cheaper oil reduces inflation pressure across the economy, which is good for your purchasing power and makes the Federal Reserve less likely to raise interest rates.
New Fed Chair Warsh takes over with no rate cuts in sight Kevin Warsh was sworn in as the new Chair of the Federal Reserve (the US central bank that sets interest rates) last Friday. He inherits a tough situation: inflation is running at 3.8% (above the Fed's 2% target), and the 30-year Treasury yield is above 5%. Markets now expect no interest rate cuts in 2026, and some traders are even pricing in a possible rate increase. For you and Maria, this means borrowing stays expensive and bonds remain under some pressure, though falling oil prices could help inflation ease over time.
S&P 500 closed at a record last Friday, extending its 8-week winning streak The S&P 500 closed at 7,473 on Friday, capping its eighth straight weekly gain, its longest winning streak since late 2023. That is good news for the stock portion of your retirement accounts. The streak has been driven by strong corporate earnings (company profits), easing trade tensions, and now the Iran deal hopes. The question now is whether the streak can continue if the Iran deal falls through or if inflation proves stubborn.

What to Watch Today

  • No major scheduled economic data releases today, so market moves will be driven by Iran deal headlines. Any breakdown in talks could reverse the morning gains quickly.
  • Watch the 10-year Treasury yield. If it stays around 4.51% or falls further, that gives a small lift to bond prices. If it climbs back toward 4.60% or above, bonds face renewed pressure.
  • Oil prices are the key signal today. If WTI crude holds below $92, that keeps inflation fears at bay and supports the current stock rally. A sudden spike back above $95 would raise alarm bells for markets.
