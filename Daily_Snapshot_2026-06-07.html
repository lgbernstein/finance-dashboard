Daily Snapshot — June 7, 2026

For Larry and Maria  |  Prepared by Claude  |  7:00 AM
Stocks fell hard Friday and closed their first losing week in 10 weeks. A strong jobs report pushed interest rates up and a chip-stock selloff dragged the Nasdaq down 4.2%. This matters because higher rates pressure both stock and bond prices in your accounts.

Where Things Stand This Weekend

Markets are closed Saturday and Sunday, so there is no live trading today. Here is where the week ended and what is coming.

Friday was a rough day. The S&P 500, the index of 500 large US companies that tracks the broad market, fell 2.6% to 7,383.74. The Dow Jones Industrial Average, an index of 30 big-name companies, dropped 1.3%. The Nasdaq, which is heavy on technology stocks, fell 4.2%, its worst day since early 2025. For the week, the S&P 500 lost 2.6% and broke a 9-week winning streak.

Two things drove the drop. First, the May jobs report came in much stronger than expected. Second, a weak forecast from chipmaker Broadcom triggered a wide selloff in semiconductor stocks. When stock futures reopen Sunday evening, they will give the first read on whether the selling continues into the new week.

Top Stories This Weekend

A strong jobs report pushed interest rates higher The economy added 172,000 jobs in May, roughly double what forecasters expected, and unemployment held at 4.3%. A strong job market sounds like good news, and it is for workers. But it makes the Federal Reserve, the US central bank that sets short-term interest rates, less likely to cut rates soon. The 10-year Treasury yield, the interest rate on US government debt that guides mortgage and bond rates, jumped to 4.54%. When yields rise, the price of bonds you already own falls.
Chip stocks led a tech selloff Broadcom, a major chipmaker, gave a weaker forecast for AI chip demand. That spooked investors across the semiconductor sector and pulled down the whole tech-heavy Nasdaq. Tech stocks had led the market to record highs this spring, so a pullback there hits the broad index hard. If your retirement funds hold large index or technology funds, expect to see this reflected when statements update.
Oil and energy prices stay elevated Ongoing conflict in the Middle East keeps oil prices high, with Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, near $86 a barrel versus $69 a year ago. Higher energy costs feed into inflation, the rate at which prices rise across the economy. That keeps pressure on the Fed to hold rates higher for longer.

What to Watch This Week

  • Wednesday, June 10: the Consumer Price Index, or CPI, the main monthly measure of inflation. Forecasters expect the annual rate to climb to about 4.2%. A hotter number would push rates up further and pressure both stocks and bonds.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield, now at 4.54%. If it keeps climbing, bond prices stay under pressure and rate-sensitive stocks struggle. If it settles, markets may calm.
  • Whether Friday's tech selloff was a one-day reaction or the start of a broader pullback. Watch how chip stocks open the week.
